    Hack in Hills ’26 · Problem 03

    One metal. Four prices.

    MCX lists four gold futures that differ only in size and purity: a 100 g bar, a 10 g bar, an 8 g coin and a 1 g piece. Per gram of pure gold, they should cost the same.

    Do they? And when they don’t, can anyone profit?

    Per gram of pure gold · {{quoteDay}}

    They nearly match.

    On {{quoteDay}} the four closed within {{spreadBp}} bp of each other. Gaps like this open and close every day.

      Check 1 · the cost line

      A gap has to pay for the trade.

      Buying one contract and selling another costs fees, taxes and slippage, in and out: {{roundTrip}} bp at 5 bp slippage a leg. Parity sets the bar at twice that, {{costLine}} bp.

        Check 2 · is it unusual?

        And it has to be rare for that contract.

        GOLDPETAL’s premium swings widely from day to day. Its {{petalGap}} bp gap is only {{petalZ}}σ from its usual level. An alert needs ±{{thr}}σ, which for GOLDPETAL means {{petalBand}} bp.

        Both checks must pass.
